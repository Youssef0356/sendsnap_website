Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about SendSnap's offline peer-to-peer sharing technology.

What is SendSnap and how does it work?

SendSnap is a premier offline peer-to-peer file transfer app designed for Android. It sets up an ultra-fast local socket connection between two devices over local Wi-Fi or Wi-Fi hotspot. Files are streamed directly between devices without ever traveling across the internet.

Do I need an active internet connection or mobile data plan?

No! SendSnap is 100% offline. You do not need mobile data, an internet connection, or cellular reception. Both devices just need to be on the same local Wi-Fi network or connected via portable Wi-Fi hotspot.

What is the maximum transfer speed?

Depending on your hardware and Wi-Fi capability (such as 5GHz Wi-Fi or Wi-Fi 6), SendSnap achieves speeds of up to 100 MB/s (800 Mbps). That means a 2 GB 4K video can transfer in under 25 seconds.

Are my files private and secure?

Completely. Because SendSnap operates entirely peer-to-peer over your local network, your personal photos, videos, and confidential documents never touch any cloud servers or external databases.

What permissions does SendSnap require and why?

SendSnap asks only for the permissions necessary to transfer files: Camera (to scan the receiver's QR code), Storage/Media access (to let you select files and save received downloads to your device), and Wi-Fi access (to establish the local P2P transfer socket). We never access your photos or files without your direct action.

Does SendSnap work on Windows PC?

Yes! SendSnap has a dedicated standalone desktop app for Windows (64-bit). You can download it directly from this website. It lets you send and receive files between your PC and Android devices over local Wi-Fi or hotspot with zero configuration.

How does multi-file sending work?

When multiple files are selected, SendSnap displays a cards container. You can drop individual file cards to different nearby devices, or grab the "DRAG ALL" master handle to send the entire batch in one gesture.

Is there any file size limit?

No. You can send files of any size—from small documents to massive multi-gigabyte video libraries or system archives. The only limit is the free storage capacity on the receiving device.
